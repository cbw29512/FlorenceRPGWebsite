Character sheet, translated

Every box means something. You only need to know when to use it.

This page uses Mara Stonehand, a simple 2014-rules level-2 fighter, to show where the important numbers live. Each numbered section explains what the player sees, what the number means, and what a DM might say when that part of the sheet matters.

What the DM actually says

Turn the boxes into table play.

The player does not need to announce a rule first. The DM hears the intent, decides what applies, and points the player to the relevant number.

2 + 5

Ability score → skill check

Player: “I try to force the stuck portcullis up.”

DM: “Make a Strength (Athletics) check, DC 13.”

Mara: Rolls d20 + Athletics 5. Her raw Strength modifier is +3, but Athletics is +5 because she is proficient.

4

Saving throw

DM: “The spider bites you. Make a Constitution saving throw, DC 12.”

Mara: Finds Constitution save +5, rolls the d20, and adds 5.

DM: Applies the effect's success or failure result.

7

Initiative

DM: “Combat begins. Everyone roll initiative.”

Mara: Rolls d20 + 2.

DM: Ranks every total highest to lowest. Highest acts first; the same order repeats each round.

6

Armor Class

DM: “The goblin rolls a 15 to hit Mara.”

Mara's AC is 17, so 15 misses. The player usually does not roll defense; the attacker rolls against AC.

10

Attack and damage

Player: “Mara attacks the goblin with her longsword.”

Mara: Rolls d20 + 5. If the total meets the goblin's AC, she then rolls 1d8 + 3 damage.

9

Hit Points

DM: “Mara takes 8 slashing damage.”

Player: Changes HP from 22 to 14.

HP tracks how much damage a character can take before 0-HP rules become relevant.

11

Class feature

Player: “Can I do anything else? I really need this goblin down.”

DM: “Check your features. You still have Action Surge.”

Mara can use it to take an additional action on that turn, then marks the resource as spent until it is recovered by the applicable rest rule.

12

Equipment

Player: “Can I throw something at the fleeing goblin?”

DM: “You have javelins on your sheet.”

The equipment list answers whether the character actually has the item needed for the plan.

The fastest way to read any sheet

Ask five questions.

  1. What am I trying to do?Describe the action in normal language.
  2. Did the DM ask for a roll?If not, do not invent one.
  3. What bonus did the DM name?Skill, save, Initiative, attack bonus, spell attack, or another printed number.
  4. What am I rolling against?A DC, an Armor Class, or another rule-defined target.
  5. What changes afterward?HP, position, a resource, a clue, a condition, or the story itself.
