Break down the character sheet

The core rules in plain English

Most of D&D follows one simple loop.

The DM describes the situation. You describe what your character tries. If the outcome is uncertain and the result matters, a d20 roll often decides what happens.

d20

Ability checks

The DM names an ability or skill and a Difficulty Class. Roll a d20, add the bonus shown on your sheet, and compare the total to the DC.

d20 + modifier ≥ DC

Example: 9 + Investigation 4 = 13 vs DC 12 → success.

+PB

Proficiency

Proficiency means your character is trained. On a finished sheet, that bonus is normally already included in proficient skills, saves, weapons, and spell numbers.

You usually use the printed total; you do not add proficiency a second time.

↑2

Advantage

Roll two d20s and use the higher die, then add the normal modifier.

roll 2d20 → keep higher

Roll 6 and 15 → use 15.

↓2

Disadvantage

Roll two d20s and use the lower die, then add the normal modifier.

roll 2d20 → keep lower

Roll 6 and 15 → use 6.

INIT

Initiative

When combat starts, roll a d20 and add your Initiative bonus—normally your Dexterity modifier unless a rule changes it. The DM ranks everyone from highest total to lowest.

highest total acts first

17 goes before 14, then 12, then 10. The same order repeats next round.

SAVE

Saving throws

A save resists something happening to you: a trap, poison, spell, blast, or similar danger. The DM tells you which save and the DC.

d20 + save modifier ≥ DC

Dex save: 7 + 5 = 12 vs DC 12 → success.

AC

Attacks & Armor Class

For an attack roll, use the attack bonus printed beside the weapon or spell. Meet or beat the target's AC to hit, then roll the listed damage.

d20 + attack bonus ≥ AC

Mara: d20 + 5 vs AC 15.

HP

Hit Points

Damage reduces HP. At 0 HP, a player character usually falls unconscious and may begin death saving throws. Healing that restores HP can bring them back.

22 HP − 8 damage = 14 HP remaining.

✦

Spells

Some spells make an attack roll. Others force a target to make a saving throw against the caster's spell save DC. The spell tells you which procedure to use.

Elira: spell attack +5; spell save DC 13.

Example one-shot

The Lost Seal of Blackstone Mine

The mayor's ceremonial silver seal disappeared after goblins were seen near an abandoned mine. Recover it before tomorrow's town gathering.

1

Meet at the inn

The heroes meet at the Lantern & Ladle. The mayor arrives, explains the missing seal, and offers a reward.

RoleplayQuest hookParty introductions
Rule: You can simply talk.

Most roleplay needs no roll. Say what your character says or describe the approach. The DM answers as the NPC. Dice enter only when the outcome is meaningfully uncertain.

Mara: “What exactly did the thieves take?”
DM: The mayor answers. No check required.
2

Ask around town

The blacksmith saw muddy footprints headed north. A shepherd heard metal clanging near the mine. A tavern regular insists witches did it.

InvestigationNPCsClues
Rule: Ability checks resolve uncertain attempts.

Tess studies the muddy tracks. The DM asks for an Intelligence (Investigation) check, DC 12. Tess looks at her sheet: Investigation +4.

Tess rolls 9 + 4 = 13. She succeeds and notices small boot prints mixed with ore dust leading toward Blackstone Mine.

A failed check does not have to stop the adventure. It can cost time, reveal less, create danger, or move the clue somewhere else.

3

Travel to the old mine

A washed-out trail ends at a broken footbridge. The party needs to cross before continuing toward the mine.

ExplorationTeamworkAdvantageDisadvantage
Rule: Circumstances can change how you roll.

Mara braces a rope while Tess crosses. The DM decides the help gives Tess advantage.

Tess rolls 6 and 15. Advantage keeps 15, then she adds the normal modifier.

If the bridge were slick with oil and the DM imposed disadvantage instead, the same rolls would keep 6.

Advantage and disadvantage change which d20 result you use; they do not replace your normal modifier.

4

The mine fights back

Inside, a pressure plate releases falling rubble while Tess is examining the floor.

TrapSaving throwDamage
Rule: Saving throws resist danger happening to you.

The DM says, “Tess, make a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw.” Tess does not choose a skill; she finds Dexterity save +5 on her sheet.

Tess rolls 7 + 5 = 12. Meeting the DC succeeds, so she dives clear. If the trap said a failed save deals 6 damage, a total of 11 would fail and she would take that listed effect.
5

Goblins!

A patrol blocks the passage. Talking breaks down and weapons come out. Combat begins.

InitiativeTurn orderCombatAC
Rule: Initiative tells everyone when they act.

Everyone rolls d20 + Initiative bonus. The DM puts the totals in order from highest to lowest. The highest total takes the first turn, then the next highest, all the way to the lowest. When the round ends, start again at the top in the same order.

17 Tess14 Mara12 Goblins10 Elira6 Alden

Initiative is normally based on Dexterity. Some features can change or improve it, so use the Initiative bonus printed on the character sheet.

6

The goblin chief

Deep in the mine, the chief wears the silver seal on his belt. Several frightened goblins watch from behind overturned crates.

Boss sceneSpellsChoices
Rule: Your sheet gives you options, not a script.
  • Mara can attack, move, use a feature, help, dodge, or attempt another reasonable action.
  • Tess can look for a Sneak Attack opportunity, hide, scout, or improvise.
  • Alden can attack, heal, support, or cast a spell.
  • Elira can use a spell attack, force a saving throw, or solve the problem without damage.
  • The whole party can negotiate, distract, threaten, retreat, surrender, or invent another plan.
7

A hero goes down

Imagine Mara reaches 0 HP during the final fight. She falls unconscious while the others scramble to help.

0 HPDeath savesHealing
Rule: 0 HP is dangerous, but it is not automatically death.

At the start of her turns while dying, Mara makes a death saving throw. Three successes stabilize her; three failures mean death. A natural 20 restores 1 HP, while a natural 1 counts as two failures.

Edition details can vary; use the death-save rules for the ruleset your table is playing.

8

Catch your breath

The chief is defeated or outmaneuvered, the seal is recovered, and the party finds a safe chamber before heading home.

Short restResourcesRecovery
Rule: Adventures are about managing resources.

Characters spend HP, Hit Dice, spell slots, class features, items, and other resources. Resting restores only what the relevant rules say it restores. The party decides whether the time and safety are worth it.

9

Return to the mayor

The party brings the seal home. The mayor pays the reward, the inn celebrates, and someone mentions strange lights at the abandoned watchtower.

RewardAdvancementNext adventure
BAM — you finished a one-shot.
  • The quest had a beginning, middle, climax, and ending.
  • Your choices changed how the scenes unfolded.
  • The DM can award treasure and advancement appropriate to the campaign.
  • The watchtower hook can become the first thread of a longer game.

What you actually need to remember

You do not need to memorize D&D before playing D&D.

Your character sheet stores the numbers. The DM tells you when a roll is needed. You describe what you want to do, find the relevant bonus, roll, and learn the rules as they become relevant.

Describe intent first.

“I check the doorway for a trap” is more useful than “I roll Investigation.” The DM decides whether a roll is needed and what applies.

Use the printed bonus.

If the sheet says Investigation +4 or Longsword +5, that number already includes the normal pieces used to calculate it.

The DM is not your opponent.

The DM portrays the world, applies the rules, presents consequences, and helps the entire table create an exciting game.
