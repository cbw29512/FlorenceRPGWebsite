D&D 5e beginner walkthrough

Play through your first adventure before you ever sit at the table.

Follow four heroes through a complete one-shot. Along the way, we will stop exactly when a rule matters and show you what the player rolls, what the Dungeon Master decides, and what happens next.

The rules in about two minutes

Most of D&D follows one simple loop.

The Dungeon Master describes the situation. You say what your character tries. If success is uncertain, dice help decide what happens.

d20

Checks

Roll a d20, add the relevant modifier, and compare the total with a Difficulty Class (DC) chosen by the DM.

d20 + modifier ≥ DC
↟

Proficiency

If your character is trained in the relevant skill, weapon, or save, their proficiency bonus is normally already included in the bonus on the sheet.

2d20

Advantage

With advantage, roll two d20s and use the higher result. With disadvantage, use the lower result.

AC

Attacks

Roll d20 + attack bonus. If the total meets or beats the target's Armor Class, the attack hits and you roll damage.

♥

Hit Points

Damage reduces HP. Reaching 0 HP usually knocks a player character unconscious and can begin death saving throws.

✦

Spells

Some spells use an attack roll. Others make the target roll a saving throw against the caster's spell save DC. Read the spell to know which.

Example one-shot

The Lost Seal of Blackstone Mine

The mayor's ceremonial silver seal disappeared after goblins were seen near an abandoned mine. Recover it before tomorrow's town gathering.

1

Meet at the inn

The heroes meet at the Lantern & Ladle. The mayor arrives, explains the missing seal, and offers a reward.

RoleplayQuest hookParty introductions
Rule: You can simply talk.

Most roleplay needs no roll. Players speak in character or describe what they say. The DM answers as the NPC. Dice enter only when the outcome is meaningfully uncertain.

Mara: “What exactly did the thieves take?”
DM: The mayor answers. No check required.
2

Ask around town

The blacksmith saw muddy footprints headed north. A shepherd heard metal clanging near the mine. A tavern regular insists witches did it.

InvestigationNPCsRed herrings
Rule: Ability checks resolve uncertain attempts.

Tess studies the muddy tracks. The DM asks for an Intelligence (Investigation) check and sets DC 12.

Tess rolls 9 + 4 = 13. Success. She notices small boot prints and ore dust leading toward Blackstone Mine.

A failed check should not always stop the adventure. It can cost time, reveal less information, or create a complication instead.

3

Travel to the old mine

A washed-out trail ends at a broken footbridge. The party needs to cross before continuing toward the mine.

ExplorationTeamworkAdvantage
Rule: Good ideas can change the roll.

Mara braces a rope while Tess crosses. The DM decides that this help gives Tess advantage on the check.

Tess rolls 6 and 15. With advantage she uses 15, adds her modifier, and crosses safely.

Advantage means two d20s, keep the higher. Disadvantage means keep the lower.

4

The mine fights back

Inside, a pressure plate releases falling rubble. Tess noticed something odd but steps too close while examining it.

TrapSaving throwDamage
Rule: Saving throws resist danger happening to you.

The DM calls for a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw. Tess rolls d20 + her Dexterity save modifier.

Tess rolls 7 + 5 = 12. She succeeds and dives clear. A failed save might deal damage or cause another listed effect.
5

Goblins!

A patrol blocks the passage. Talking fails and weapons come out. Combat begins.

InitiativeCombatACDamage
Rule: Combat happens in rounds and turns.

Everyone rolls initiative. On your turn you can move up to your Speed and take an action. Some characters also have something that lets them take a bonus action. Reactions happen only when a rule gives you a trigger for one.

Try the dice

Play one round of the goblin fight.

This is a teaching simulator, not the full D&D combat engine. Roll the same kinds of checks you would make at the table.

ReadyStart with initiative. The d20 decides plenty, but your modifiers represent what your character is good at.

Attack roll

Mara's example attack is d20 + 5 against AC 15. Meeting or beating AC hits.

Damage

On a normal hit, this example rolls 1d8 + 3. Damage reduces the target's hit points.

Critical hit

A natural 20 on an attack hits critically. Roll the attack's damage dice twice, then add modifiers once.

Natural 1

A natural 1 on an attack roll misses. Natural 1s and 20s do not automatically fail or succeed on ordinary ability checks.

6

The goblin chief

Deep in the mine, the chief wears the silver seal on his belt. Several frightened goblins watch from behind overturned crates.

Boss sceneSpellsChoices
Rule: Your character sheet gives you options, not a script.
  • Mara can charge the chief.
  • Tess can hide and look for an opening.
  • Alden can support allies or use divine magic.
  • Elira can make a spell attack or cast a spell that forces a saving throw.
  • The whole party can negotiate, distract, threaten, surrender, retreat, or invent something else.
7

A hero goes down

Imagine Mara reaches 0 HP during the final fight. She falls unconscious while the others scramble to finish the battle and help her.

0 HPDeath savesHealing
Rule: 0 HP is dangerous, but it is not automatically death.

At the start of her turns while dying, Mara makes a death saving throw: 10 or higher succeeds; 9 or lower fails. Three successes stabilize her; three failures mean death. A natural 20 restores 1 HP, while a natural 1 counts as two failures.

Healing that restores HP can bring an unconscious character back into the fight.

8

Catch your breath

The chief is defeated or outmaneuvered, the seal is recovered, and the party finds a safe chamber before making the trip home.

Short restResourcesRecovery
Rule: Adventures are about managing resources.

Characters spend hit points, spell slots, class features, items, and other resources. Resting can restore some of them depending on the rule involved. Players decide whether resting is worth the time and risk.

9

Return to the mayor

The party brings the recovered seal home. The mayor pays the promised reward, the inn celebrates, and someone mentions strange lights at the abandoned watchtower.

RewardLevel upNext adventure
BAM — you finished a one-shot.
  • The quest had a beginning, middle, climax, and ending.
  • Your decisions changed how scenes unfolded.
  • The DM can award treasure and advancement appropriate to the game.
  • A milestone can level the party when the DM decides the story has reached an appropriate point.

What you actually need to remember

You do not need to memorize D&D before playing D&D.

The character sheet holds most of your numbers. The DM tells you when a roll is needed. You say what you want to do and learn the rules as they become relevant.

Describe intent first.

“I check the doorway for a trap” is more useful than “I roll Investigation.” The DM decides whether a roll is needed and what applies.

Failure moves the story.

A poor roll can cost HP, time, position, information, resources, or create a new problem. It does not have to make the adventure stop.

The DM is not your opponent.

The DM portrays the world, applies the rules, presents consequences, and helps the entire table create an exciting game.
