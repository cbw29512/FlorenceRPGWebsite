D&D beginner walkthrough

What does a complete adventure actually look like?

A one-shot is a complete story you can play in a single sitting. You meet your party, accept a problem, investigate, explore dangerous places, make decisions, fight when necessary, overcome a final challenge, and come home changed.

Meet the party

Four ready-to-play heroes

This is your party.

You do not need to create a character before your first game. A beginner table can hand you a ready-to-play hero and teach you as the story unfolds.

F

Mara Stonehand

Fighter · Front-line protector

Good at standing between danger and the rest of the party.

Your simple job: protect friends and hit dangerous things.

R

Tess Quickstep

Rogue · Scout and problem-solver

Good at sneaking, spotting trouble, opening locks, and striking at the right moment.

Your simple job: investigate and look for clever solutions.

Cleric · Support and divine magic

Good at helping allies, healing wounds, and standing firm when things go wrong.

Example one-shot

The Lost Seal of Blackstone Mine

The mayor's ceremonial silver seal disappeared after goblins were seen near an abandoned mine. Recover it before tomorrow's town gathering.

1

Meet at the inn

The four heroes are introduced at the Lantern & Ladle Inn. The mayor arrives, explains the missing seal, and offers a reward.

RoleplayQuest hookParty introductions
What happens at the table
  • Players introduce their characters.
  • The GM describes the inn and mayor.
  • Players ask questions, joke, negotiate, or accept immediately.
2

Ask around town

The party talks to locals before leaving. The blacksmith saw muddy footprints headed north. A frightened shepherd heard metal clanging near the mine. A tavern regular insists witches did it.

InvestigationNPCsChoices
Not every clue means the same thing
  • Useful clue: tracks lead toward Blackstone Mine.
  • Red herring: the witch story is wrong.
  • Future hook: someone mentions strange lights at the old watchtower.
3

Travel to the old mine

The road ends at a washed-out trail. The party crosses a broken footbridge, climbs a loose rock shelf, and follows tracks toward the mine entrance.

ExplorationAbility checksTeamwork
The GM presents problems, not answers
  • The fighter might brace a rope.
  • The rogue might find a safer route.
  • The wizard might solve the obstacle with magic.
  • A failed roll creates a complication instead of ending the adventure.
4

The mine fights back

Inside, the party finds a hidden snare, unstable timbers, and a dark side passage. Careful players can spot the danger. Reckless players may trigger it and adapt.

TrapHazardDecision
This is why different characters matter
  • Someone searches ahead.
  • Someone protects the group.
  • Someone treats injuries.
  • Everyone gets a chance to contribute.
5

Goblins!

A goblin patrol discovers the heroes near an underground storage room. The party can fight, intimidate them, attempt a trick, or try another idea the GM did not expect.

CombatTacticsImprovisation
Combat has a rhythm
  • Roll initiative to determine turn order.
  • Move and take actions on your turn.
  • Help allies and react to changing circumstances.
  • The goal is not perfect play; it is making decisions together.
6

The goblin king

Deep in the mine, the party reaches an improvised throne room. The goblin chief has the silver seal hanging from his belt and several frightened followers nearby.

Boss sceneRoleplay or combatPayoff
The finale brings earlier choices together
  • Charge in and fight.
  • Try to negotiate for the seal.
  • Create a distraction.
  • Use clues or resources found earlier.
7

Return to the mayor

The party brings the recovered seal back to town. The mayor pays the promised reward, the inn erupts in celebration, and the strange lights at the old watchtower hint at another adventure.

RewardLevel upNext adventure
BAM — you finished a one-shot
  • You completed a beginning, middle, and ending.
  • Your choices changed how the story unfolded.
  • The characters receive treasure and can advance by milestone.
  • The next game can be a new one-shot or the start of a campaign.

What you just saw

A role-playing game is a conversation with consequences.

The GM describes the world and its problems. The players decide what their characters attempt. Dice are used when the outcome is uncertain. Then everyone discovers what happens next together.

You do not memorize the rules first.

Learn the pieces you need when they appear. A good beginner game teaches while you play.

There is rarely one correct answer.

Fight the goblins, trick them, bargain with them, sneak around them, or surprise the GM with another plan.

The GM is not your opponent.

The GM presents the world, challenges, and consequences while helping the whole table create an exciting story.
